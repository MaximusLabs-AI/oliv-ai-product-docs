# Buyer, ICP and evaluation guides

*V1 review draft · 2026-10-16 · Source-described scope; confirm workspace availability*

<a id="overview"></a>
## Overview

Understand ideal-fit signals, the buying committee, company-stage scenarios and the complete 735-question buyer universe.

<a id="fit"></a>
## Practical fit signals



- A B2B revenue motion with recurring customer interactions and identifiable account or opportunity ownership.
- An existing CRM and an accountable owner for revenue definitions and field mappings.
- Permitted access to representative interaction data and a team willing to review AI proposals.
- A specific sales or post-sale workflow with a measurable baseline and named sponsor.

<a id="qualification"></a>
## Qualify the deployment before purchase

The supplied ICP materials contain several segmentation models, and the current website also has a startup offer. Treat rep, employee and ARR bands as planning context, not universal eligibility rules. Confirm actual supported segments, minimum commitments and deployment prerequisites with Oliv.

<a id="poor-fit"></a>
## Fit concerns to resolve



- An expectation that Oliv replaces the CRM conflicts with the approved complement positioning.
- No accountable CRM or revenue-process owner makes mapping and governance difficult.
- No lawful access to interactions or no review owner prevents a credible pilot.
- A requirement for a native category such as full CLM, LMS, ICM or reverse ETL needs explicit product evidence.

<a id="firmographic-lens"></a>
## Firmographic and market context

The supplied ICP research emphasizes B2B revenue organizations, especially SaaS and IT services, with multiple customer interactions and a need for consistent execution. It also discusses financial services, logistics, healthcare and other verticals as research segments. Inclusion here does not establish industry-specific compliance or implementation support.

The research discusses US-led buying alongside EMEA and APAC teams and remote or hybrid work. Confirm supported regions, recording rules, languages, data residency and communication channels for the actual deployment.

<a id="technographic-lens"></a>
## Technographic readiness



| Existing system | Why it matters | Confirmation required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CRM | Account, contact, opportunity and field ownership | Supported connector, objects, schemas and write permissions |
| Meeting and recording stack | Source interaction evidence | Organizer eligibility, capture mode, consent and recording rights |
| Calendar and email | Meeting identity and between-meeting context | User identity, read scopes, sending scopes and tenant restrictions |
| Chat and notification channels | Daily review and ownership | Recipient scope, permissions, escalation and supported actions |
| Engagement, dialer or CS systems | Additional activity and account signals | Named product, supported artifacts, freshness and licensing |
| Process definitions | Methodology and approval consistency | Accountable handbook owner, current definitions and change approval |

<a id="readiness-signals"></a>
## Behavioral readiness signals



- Leadership has chosen a specific AI workflow and accepts human review while behavior is validated.
- A process owner can resolve conflicting definitions rather than asking an agent to infer company policy.
- Managers and reps will inspect evidence and correct proposals instead of accepting every generated answer.
- The team can provide a representative pilot cohort and compare results with its current operating baseline.

<a id="buying-triggers"></a>
## Buying triggers and the first decision



<a id="trigger-forecast"></a>
### Repeated forecast misses

Reconcile past submissions and actuals. Determine whether the issue is stale CRM data, inconsistent definitions, missing deal evidence or judgment. Evaluate Forecaster against that diagnosed problem rather than a universal accuracy promise.

<a id="trigger-data"></a>
### CRM hygiene and administration

Identify unreliable fields and activities and their owners. Test proposed updates, rejected suggestions and CRM validation rules before widening write access.

<a id="trigger-renewal"></a>
### Existing vendor renewal

Evaluate actual workflow value and total cost. Compare current quotes and requirements; historical competitor prices in the master are not purchasing evidence.

<a id="trigger-scale"></a>
### Team growth and process inconsistency

Document stages, ownership, qualification and handoffs before expanding. Test that multiple managers and teams interpret the same process consistently.

<a id="trigger-coaching"></a>
### Uneven rep development

Agree the skill or behavior to improve, a reviewed scorecard and manager involvement. Inspect call examples and progress rather than assuming a score causes higher win rates.

<a id="trigger-retention"></a>
### Renewal risk and missed expansion

Reconcile renewal data and account ownership. Review risk and growth signals with CS and sales before creating opportunities or customer messages.

<a id="trigger-ai"></a>
### Leadership AI initiative or build-versus-buy review

Bound the required workflow, data and controls. Compare maintenance and governance effort for the actual options; the master’s content plans are not measured product comparisons.

<a id="qualification-checklist"></a>
## Qualification questions



1. What recurring revenue workflow creates the greatest measurable operating burden?
2. Which CRM and source systems own the necessary records and interactions?
3. Who can authorize access, define the process and review outputs?
4. Which approval, security, legal and procurement requirements are non-negotiable?
5. Which result and exception case would prove this workflow useful?
6. Which required capability lacks evidence in the evaluation map?
7. What is the approved seat, credit, implementation and renewal cost for this deployment?
